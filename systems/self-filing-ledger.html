WHY IT HAS TO EXIST

The last mile of accounting
was never built.

Small companies do simple books and owe simple filings — yet the path from one to the other runs through subscriptions, portals and professional fees that cost more than the accounting itself. Four failures, one gap.

F·01

Software stops at the PDF

Bookkeeping tools produce reports, exports and dashboards — then hand you back the actual obligation. The statutory filing, the one thing the company legally owes, is left as an exercise for the reader.

F·02

The free lanes are closing

Government self-filing services are being retired in favour of software-only submission — in the UK, both the registry's web filing for accounts and the tax authority's free company return service are on the way out. Every small company will be pushed into commercial software whether it wants it or not.

F·03

Priced for practices, not companies

Filing software is built for accountancy firms processing hundreds of clients, and priced that way. A single company with twelve transactions a year is offered the same seat licence as a practice — or nothing.

F·04

Simple companies pay complex fees

A dormant or micro company owes a confirmation statement and a set of near-empty accounts. Paying professional rates every year to restate that nothing happened is the clearest market failure in small-company life.

WHAT IT CHANGES

From records
to receipts.

The measure of an accounting system isn't the report it renders — it's the obligation it discharges. The Self-Filing Ledger is judged on one thing: returns filed, evidence in hand.

One continuous line.

Transactions to trial balance to statutory accounts to submission — one system, no export, no re-keying, no handover to a portal.

Deadlines that watch you.

The ledger knows every filing the company owes in its jurisdiction — confirmation statements, accounts, tax returns — and runs the calendar so the director doesn't have to.

Proof, not promises.

Every submission returns a registry acknowledgment, stored against the filing. The system's word is never the evidence — the stamped receipt is.

THE MECHANICS

Four layers,
one machine.

Built the VF way: a deterministic core doing the arithmetic, models doing the judgement work inside a harness, and a gate in front of anything that leaves the building.

LAYER 1

Ledger core

A double-entry engine that runs local-first on the owner's machine. Deterministic arithmetic, full audit trail, no cloud dependency — the books never leave the building.

LAYER 2

Mapping engine

The judgement layer: open models classify transactions and map the trial balance onto each jurisdiction's statutory format and digital taxonomy. Model-assisted, never model-trusted — every mapping traces back to source entries.

LAYER 3

Filing adapters

One adapter per authority — company registry and tax office, per country — speaking each system's native submission protocol. Jurisdictions are plug-ins: the UK first, Malta and Germany on our own books next.

LAYER 4

The gate

The VF harness discipline applied to accounts: no figure files unless it reconciles to the ledger, and no return leaves without passing evaluation. Failed checks stop the filing — not the other way round.
