WHY IT HAS TO EXIST

Every EU adult gets a wallet.
Someone should build a good one.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1183 mandates that every member state offers a digital identity wallet to every resident by end-2026, with a target of 80% adoption by 2030. This is real, EU-level regulation, actively rolling out. Four failure modes are already visible in the national wallets pilot phase. One gap, filled by the private-sector alternative the Regulation itself permits.

F·01

Built to comply, not to compete

National wallets are contracted to systems integrators + delivered as government IT projects. They will pass conformance and they will be functional. Nobody is losing sleep over their UI.

F·02

The government logs what you present

Some national implementations retain server-side presentation logs — some for operational reasons, some by design. Users have no export path and no visibility of what's kept.

F·03

No serious answer if the vendor quits

When a government wallet is retired or replaced, the users get whatever migration the state chooses. Historical precedent is not reassuring. Belkin killed Wemo in a Milan press release; states can be equally sudden.

F·04

Cross-border is theory, until you try it

The Trust List federates by architecture, but user-experience quirks between DE and FR and IE and IT are real. Nobody's UX team has your Malta-EU-diaspora edge case as a priority.

THE MECHANICS

Four layers,
one honest wallet.

R·01 ships as a Rust core with FFI bindings to Swift and Kotlin, a thin backend that never sees plaintext, and a reference verifier for third-party integrators. Every subsystem uses well-studied open primitives (Ed25519, X25519, Argon2id, XChaCha20-Poly1305, BLAKE3) plus two new sub-primitives added to `vf-privacy-kit` — `zk-age-proof` and `wsc-attestation`.

LAYER 1

App shell

Native SwiftUI on iOS 17+; Kotlin/Compose on Android 12+ including GrapheneOS and /e/OS — zero Google Play Services dependency. The user-facing surface: consent screens, presentation flows, audit-log inspection, panic-wipe.

LAYER 2

Audit log ring

Append-only SQLite table on device, BLAKE3-chained, WSCD-signed. Every presentation recorded with the verifier's purpose statement verbatim, fields disclosed, session binding. Exportable as PDF or JSON. Included in the sealed sync blob for backup. Destroyed by panic-wipe.

LAYER 3

Credential vault

SQLite database encrypted at rest with XChaCha20-Poly1305. Key derived from PIN via Argon2id (t=3, m=64 MiB, p=4). Stores credentials in their native format — mdoc for ISO 18013-7, SD-JWT-VC for attestations, zero-knowledge proofs for age verification.

LAYER 4

WSCD kernel

Wallet Secure Cryptographic Device. Apple Secure Enclave on iOS. Android StrongBox Keymaster on Android. Certified external hardware (Yubikey NFC) as fallback. Signing keys never leave hardware. Attestation to verifiers on request.
