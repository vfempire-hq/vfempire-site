WHY IT HAS TO EXIST

The hardware revolution happened.
The intelligence never arrived.

Solar, batteries, heat pumps, EVs: the equipment is on the wall and in the driveway. The ordinary household lacks coordination, the layer that decides, hour by hour, what should run, charge, store or sell.

F·01

Self-consumption runs low

Unmanaged solar homes typically use only around 20–40% of what their own roof generates; with storage and smart scheduling, studies show that share can rise dramatically. The gap between those numbers is pure coordination, and at present no one does it.

F·02

The grid pays for the chaos

Germany spent roughly €2.8 billion on grid stabilisation measures in 2024, with over a terawatt-hour of solar curtailed. Millions of uncoordinated homes are part of the problem; coordinated, they are part of the answer.

F·03

Dynamic pricing is invisible

Surveys find more than half of German households have never heard of dynamic electricity tariffs. The price signal exists; without an agent watching it, it might as well not.

F·04

Every vendor built a silo

Inverter app, battery app, wallbox app, heat-pump app, none of them talking to each other. Five million German PV systems and a million balcony plants, each an island of good hardware with no orchestration.

WHAT IT CHANGES

The house decides hourly.
You just live in it.

The Optimiser watches forecasted sun, tariff windows and household patterns, and schedules the controllable loads locally and continuously, without asking the household to become energy traders.

Own power, used.

The Optimiser pushes self-consumption toward what the hardware can deliver, routing the roof’s output into the house’s real needs instead of exporting it for cents.

Peak prices, avoided.

It moves shiftable loads (water, heat, EV, battery) into cheap and sunny windows on its own, with tariff awareness the household never has to think about.

Grid-friendly by design.

A coordinated home smooths its own peaks, which is easier on the local grid and ready for the flexible-consumption frameworks Europe is rolling out.

THE MECHANICS

Local brain.
Open connectors. Your metal.

The architecture is the VF standard: intelligence running on a hub in the house, vendor-neutral connectors to whatever equipment is installed, and a household that owns every byte of its own energy life.

Every VF system runs on the harness stack, is steered by the Helmsman engine and is watched from a command dashboard.

LAYER 1

The device layer

Vendor-neutral connectors to inverters, batteries, wallboxes, heat pumps and meters over open protocols wherever they exist. Mixed-vendor homes are the design case.

LAYER 2

The forecast core

Solar yield from weather models and the roof’s own history, consumption patterns learned per household, tariff calendars ingested per supplier.

LAYER 3

The scheduling engine

Rolling optimisation that plans the next hours (charge, hold, run, defer) and re-solves as forecasts shift. Comfort and user overrides are hard constraints; savings never come out of warm showers.

LAYER 4

The household surface

One app for the whole energy life: live flows, the plan, the receipts of what the coordination achieved. It replaces five vendor silo apps with one owned view.

QUESTIONS

Asked.
Answered.

Which equipment does it work with?

Inverters, batteries, wallboxes, heat pumps and meters over open protocols wherever they exist, through vendor-neutral connectors. Mixed-vendor homes are the design case, and device connectors will be open so no household is locked out because of its inverter brand. Pilot homes define the connector priority list.

Does it run in the cloud?

No. The intelligence runs on a hub in the house, with vendor-neutral connectors to whatever equipment is installed, and the household owns every byte of its own energy life. One app replaces the five vendor silo apps with a single owned view of live flows, the plan and the receipts of what the coordination achieved.

What does the Optimiser do?

It watches forecasted sun, tariff windows and household patterns and schedules the controllable loads locally and continuously. It pushes self-consumption toward what the hardware can deliver, moves shiftable loads (water, heat, EV, battery) into cheap and sunny windows, and smooths the home’s own peaks. Comfort and user overrides are hard constraints.

Will it turn my heating down to save money?

No. Comfort and user overrides are hard constraints in the scheduling engine, and savings never come out of warm showers. Heating is the biggest load in the house and the most shiftable, so the Optimiser schedules heat against forecast and price with comfort held fixed.

When does it ship?

Grid & Home Optimiser is in build. The first homes phase puts hub software on pilot households: connectors for common EU equipment, solar and load forecasting, and first closed-loop scheduling of battery and major loads. Dynamic-tariff integration, heat-pump scheduling, EV charging and balcony-plant mode follow, then general availability with installer partnerships.
