WHY IT HAS TO EXIST

The hardware revolution happened.
The intelligence never arrived.

Solar, batteries, heat pumps, EVs — the equipment is on the wall and in the driveway. What is missing in the ordinary household is coordination: the layer that decides, hour by hour, what should run, charge, store or sell.

F·01

Self-consumption runs low

Unmanaged solar homes typically use only around 20–40% of what their own roof generates; with storage and smart scheduling, studies show that share can rise dramatically. The gap between those numbers is pure coordination — currently done by nobody.

F·02

The grid pays for the chaos

Germany spent roughly €2.8 billion on grid stabilisation measures in 2024, with over a terawatt-hour of solar curtailed. Millions of uncoordinated homes are part of the problem; coordinated, they are part of the answer.

F·03

Dynamic pricing is invisible

Surveys find more than half of German households have never even heard of dynamic electricity tariffs. The price signal exists; without an agent watching it, it might as well not.

F·04

Every vendor built a silo

Inverter app, battery app, wallbox app, heat-pump app — none of them talking. Five million German PV systems and a million balcony plants, each an island of good hardware and absent orchestration.

WHAT IT CHANGES

The house decides hourly.
You just live in it.

The Optimiser watches forecasted sun, tariff windows and household patterns, and schedules the controllable loads — locally, continuously, without asking the household to become energy traders.

Own power, used.

Self-consumption pushed toward what the hardware can actually deliver — the roof’s output routed into the house’s real needs instead of exported for cents.

Peak prices, avoided.

Shiftable loads — water, heat, EV, battery — moved into cheap and sunny windows automatically, with tariff awareness the household never has to think about.

Grid-friendly by design.

A coordinated home smooths its own peaks — easier on the local grid and ready for the flexible-consumption frameworks Europe is rolling out.

THE MECHANICS

Local brain.
Open connectors. Your metal.

The architecture is the VF standard: intelligence running on a hub in the house, vendor-neutral connectors to whatever equipment is installed, and a household that owns every byte of its own energy life.

LAYER 1

The device layer

Vendor-neutral connectors to inverters, batteries, wallboxes, heat pumps and meters over open protocols wherever they exist. Mixed-vendor homes are the design case, not the edge case.

LAYER 2

The forecast core

Solar yield from weather models and the roof’s own history, consumption patterns learned per household, tariff calendars ingested per supplier.

LAYER 3

The scheduling engine

Rolling optimisation that plans the next hours — charge, hold, run, defer — re-solved as forecasts shift. Comfort and user overrides are hard constraints; savings never come out of warm showers.

LAYER 4

The household surface

One app for the whole energy life: live flows, the plan, the receipts of what the coordination achieved — replacing five vendor silo apps with one owned view.
