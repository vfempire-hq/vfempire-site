WHY IT HAS TO EXIST

The trades are short-handed.
And the tools went to the office.

The skilled-trades shortage is one of Europe’s most measured problems, and the knowledge burden on each remaining tradesperson keeps growing. AI landed everywhere except the van.

F·01

The shortage is structural

Germany’s ifo institute found 22.7% of firms reporting skilled-labour shortages constraining business — with construction and trades among the hardest hit. Fewer hands must now carry more knowledge, faster.

F·02

The codebook never stops moving

Electrical codes are revised on multi-year cycles — the US NEC alone turns over every three years, and every EU state maintains its own evolving norms. A career spans decades of moving regulation, and looking it up mid-job is unpaid time.

F·03

Vehicles became computers

Industry surveys report around half of automotive technicians feel underprepared for the electronics now arriving in vehicles. The diagnostic layer of a modern car outgrew the training most mechanics were given.

F·04

The paperwork eats the evening

Quotes, job reports, compliance records, photos filed to the right customer — the administrative tail of trade work is done at the kitchen table, after the billable day ends.

WHAT IT CHANGES

Knowledge at the panel,
not back at the office.

The Apprentice is an assistant, not an authority. It brings the reference material, the reasoning support and the paperwork; the judgement and the sign-off belong to the professional — legally and by design.

Diagnose with a second brain.

Symptom-driven reasoning that proposes causes and test sequences, learning the shape of each trade’s real failure patterns. The tradesperson stays the decision-maker.

The code, cited.

Regulation and norm reference with sources shown — a fast, checkable answer instead of a memory gamble or an evening of PDF archaeology.

Paperwork done on site.

Job reports, quotes and compliance documentation drafted from photos and dictation before the van door closes — the kitchen-table shift deleted.

THE MECHANICS

Built for gloves,
noise and no signal.

A job site is the hostile opposite of a desk: loud, dirty, offline in basements and plant rooms. The system is engineered from those constraints, not adapted to them later.

LAYER 1

The site interface

Voice-first and glance-based interaction on the phone the tradesperson already carries. Hands stay on the work; the copilot listens and answers.

LAYER 2

The knowledge core

Curated trade knowledge, norms and manufacturer documentation, retrieval-grounded so answers carry citations. Per-country code editions handled explicitly — never blended.

LAYER 3

The diagnostic engine

Structured reasoning over symptoms, measurements and history — hypothesis, discriminating test, next step. Confidence is stated; uncertainty is admitted.

LAYER 4

The paperwork mill

Photos, dictated notes and job data compiled into quotes, reports and compliance records in the firm’s own format — reviewed and signed by the human, always.
