WHY IT HAS TO EXIST

Millions of learners.
One settings toggle each.

The population is enormous, the support systems are buckling, and the technology response so far has been accessibility checkboxes on products designed for someone else’s attention span.

F·01

This is not an edge case

Estimates commonly place dyslexia around one in ten people, ADHD near one in twenty children, autism at one in a hundred or more. Every classroom in Europe contains these minds; “edge case” was a design excuse.

F·02

The support system is over capacity

In England, local authorities issued under half of education-and-care plans within the statutory 20-week deadline in recent data, and thousands of families waited over a year. The queue arrives before the help does.

F·03

Retrofitted, at best

Mainstream platforms add dyslexia fonts and call it inclusion. The design variables that matter for these learners (pacing, working-memory load, sensory environment, interest-driven motivation) go untouched.

F·04

The cost compounds for life

Only around three in ten autistic adults are in work against roughly eight in ten non-disabled adults. Educational experiences that fail these minds early compound into lifelong exclusion.

WHAT IT CHANGES

The material bends.
The learner doesn’t have to.

The Engine is a learning tool with a hard boundary: it adapts and teaches, it does not diagnose or screen. It changes the daily experience: content meets the mind where it is, in the mode it thinks in.

Presentation that fits.

The Engine adjusts text load, structure, sensory intensity and modality per learner, turning reading-heavy material visual, sequential, spoken or interactive as the mind requires.

Pacing that respects attention.

Session shapes built for real attention patterns (short arcs, movement breaks, hyperfocus runs) that treat the learner’s rhythm as a parameter.

Progress made visible.

Growth shown against the learner’s own baseline and shared with the adults the family chooses: evidence of a mind moving, for children who rarely get to see themselves win.

THE MECHANICS

A profile, a pedagogy,
and a hard privacy floor.

Underneath is a learner model built with specialist input, a content engine that renders one curriculum many ways, and a children’s-data architecture designed to the strictest reading of the rules, because the users are children.

LAYER 1

The learner profile

A model of how this student learns (modality, pacing, load tolerance, interests), built from observed interaction and educator input. A learning profile, never a diagnostic instrument.

LAYER 2

The adaptation engine

One curriculum, many renderings: the same concept expressed across modalities and structures, selected and sequenced against the profile in real time.

LAYER 3

The evidence loop

Progress measured against the learner’s own history, feeding both the adaptation and the human reports, with educators and specialists shaping what is measured at all.

LAYER 4

The guardian layer

Children’s-privacy architecture: data minimisation, parental control, no advertising, no profiling beyond the learning purpose, EU hosting. That is the floor.
