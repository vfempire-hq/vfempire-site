THE STATE OF SOFTWARE · 2026

Five failures
the industry stopped hiding.

Everything the modern user hates about software traces back to five business decisions the industry has openly made. Not accidents. Not bugs. Choices. Each with a receipt from the last twenty-four months.

F·01

Post-acquisition enshittification

A private-equity roll-up buys the tools you rely on and turns them hostile inside a year. Bending Spoons alone has swallowed Evernote, WeTransfer, Vimeo, Meetup, Komoot, Filmic Pro, Eventbrite and AOL — laying off around 75% of engineering on each and pushing subscription prices up 30–85% within months.

Evernote personal annual $69.99 → $129.99 · WeTransfer free tier capped at 10 uploads/month · Vimeo & Eventbrite acquired Dec 2025
F·02

Forced AI ingestion

The AI features you didn't ask for are reading the files you didn't consent to share. Google Gemini was caught silently summarising Drive documents in Nov 2025; Microsoft's Recall built a full screen-history index without user consent; Adobe rewrote its terms to allow content ingestion for training and only walked back after professional revolt.

Tinder Chemistry scanned camera rolls Nov 2025 · Microsoft dialling back Windows 11 Copilot integrations Jan 2026 after 18-month backlash
F·03

Cloud-shutdown wave

The product you bought becomes a paperweight the moment the vendor gets bored of its cloud. Belkin killed the entire Wemo smart-home line in Jan 2026; Logitech gave POP-button owners two weeks' notice; Neato robots died on their servers in 2025; 23andMe filed Chapter 11 in Mar 2025 with seven million customers' genomes listed as bankruptcy inventory.

Google's product graveyard is now 51.5% of everything they ever shipped · Plex paywalled remote streaming of your own media Apr 29 2025
F·04

Dark-pattern billing

The subscription is the product. Year one is cheap; auto-renewal is deliberately opaque. The US FTC extracted $2.5B from Amazon in Sept 2025 over Prime signup and cancellation dark patterns — the largest consumer-protection settlement in agency history. The UK CMA has done the same to major security suites.

Amazon FTC settlement Sept 2025 · CMA auto-renewal enforcement against Norton, McAfee & Bitdefender 2024-25
F·05

You are the inventory

Free apps sell your data. Paid apps still sell your data. Fitbit's own privacy policy permits employer-facing sharing; a 2025 Duke University study found 79% of fitness apps share user data with third parties; a California jury ruled in 2025 that Meta violated the state's privacy statute over Flo period-tracking data — nine-figure class exposure follows. When you don't pay, you're the inventory. When you do pay, you're often still the inventory.

Duke 2025: 79% of fitness apps share data · Flo/Meta CIPA verdict 2025 · JMIR 2025 flagged Google/Fitbit as most permissive · 107 US BIPA class actions in 2025 alone
THE VF ANSWER

Five principles.
Every VF product inherits all five.

Not features. Not marketing lines. Non-negotiable house rules — enforced by architecture (local encryption, sealed transport, signed packs), by contract (Terms § 8 warranty with refund + public post-mortem), and by the fact that we sell no ads and hold no other revenue stream that could compromise them.

Local-first

Your data lives on your hardware. Servers see only opaque sealed blobs — never plaintext.

No third-party AI

Local models or our own EU GPU inference. Never OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Cohere on user-data paths.

EU-sovereign

Malta jurisdiction, EU-only infrastructure. Two-of-two threshold signing across separate EU member states.

Subscription-only

No advertising. No data-broker relationships. No affiliate revenue. If you're not the customer, we don't build it.

Verifiable

Reproducible builds. Ed25519-signed updates. Public transparency log. Every claim we make can be independently checked.

THE HOUSE STANDARD

Trust, mechanised.
Six primitives every product inherits.

The five principles above are enforced by six shared mechanisms — the same across every VF product. If you learn them once, you know how every VF app behaves. If we ever violate one, you have a receipt in hand.

P·01

Terms § 8 warranty

A material privacy breach on our part = automatic refund + public post-mortem within 5 business days. Enforceable in Malta.

P·02

Reproducible builds

Every shipping binary is byte-for-byte reproducible from open source. Third-party CI mirrors in Berlin + Reykjavik continuously verify.

P·03

2-of-2 threshold signing

Every update, every rule pack, every model pack signed by two operators in different EU jurisdictions with physical Yubikey touch.

P·04

Transparency log

Signed updates recorded in a public RFC 6962 Merkle tree. Anyone can prove what was signed, when, by whom. Post-hoc tampering is arithmetically visible.

P·05

Bug bounty · €500 – €25 000

Design typos to novel privacy leaks. Paid out publicly with severity histogram at bounty.vfempire.com. Every payout accumulates trust across the family.

P·06

Permanence guarantee

If VF ever discontinues a product, the source is released under an open licence and the last signed firmware remains verifiable in the transparency log. Nothing you bought becomes a paperweight.

THE HOUSE-WIDE LAW

Everything we ship,
outlives us if it has to.

The single most repeated pain in software over the last two years is the vendor decided. Belkin decided to end Wemo. Logitech decided to end POP. Neato decided to end its cloud. 23andMe decided to enter Chapter 11 with seven million genomes as inventory. VF makes one commitment, in writing, that answers this in advance.

If VF discontinues a product, the source is opened, the last signed firmware remains verifiable in the transparency log, and the sealed vault format stays readable — permanently.

This is not a marketing promise. It is a Terms § 8 clause. Enforceable in Malta courts. Backed by an insurance-funded escrow for the source-release lane. And baked into the reproducibility guarantee: because every shipping binary is byte-reproducible from open source, the source drop actually reconstructs the product you bought.

Full source code released under a permissive open-source licence within 90 days of any end-of-life announcement. Escrow-backed across two EU jurisdictions.

THE FIRMWARE

Verifiable forever

THE DATA
