VF Mail vs Proton MailTwo encrypted mail systems, compared on architecture

VF Mail is a complete mail system (server, deliverability shield and address) that VF Empire runs on metal it holds in Malta, EU, with end-to-end encryption between VF users and keys that never leave your hardware. Proton Mail is an encrypted email service operated by Proton AG from Switzerland. This page compares the two on where mail is stored, who holds the keys, protocols, business model, open-source status and jurisdiction, and marks where VF Mail is still in beta.

DEFINITIONS

What each one is.
In one paragraph.

Both services exist because the mainstream inbox lives inside an advertising ecosystem. They answer that problem with different architectures, and the architecture is what this page compares.

P·16 · VF EMPIRE

VF Mail

VF Mail is the full stack: transport, storage, shield and interface. A memory-safe mail server of the Stalwart generation runs on clean-reputation metal in EU jurisdiction with SPF, DKIM, DMARC, MTA-STS and DANE. VF runs the deliverability operation (IP warming, monitoring, blocklist response) as a managed discipline, so you never fight that war yourself.

The first @vfempire.com inboxes already run on VF's own node. The Windows and Linux builds are downloadable now and free while in Early Access; the paid launch is November 1. Mail between VF users is end-to-end encrypted, direct messages are encrypted, and the client ships with no signup, no telemetry and no analytics.

Source: the VF Mail product page and the security posture.
PROTON AG · SWITZERLAND

Proton Mail

Proton Mail is an encrypted email service from Proton AG, a company headquartered in Geneva that describes itself as operating under Swiss law. As Proton describes in its documentation, messages are stored with what it calls zero-access encryption, mail between Proton accounts is end-to-end encrypted by default, and private keys are encrypted with the user's password and unlocked in the client.

Proton publishes the source code of its client applications, offers a free tier alongside paid subscriptions, and states that it funds the service through subscriptions. Proton has said that the company is majority-owned by the non-profit Proton Foundation. The service has operated since 2014.

Source: Proton's own public documentation and announcements. VF makes no claim about Proton's security beyond what Proton states.

THE DECISION

Choose by architecture,
and by what you need today.

Choose Proton Mail if

  • You need a generally available service today, with a free tier, on every platform.
  • You want a provider under Swiss law, outside both EU and US jurisdiction.
  • You already correspond with Proton users and rely on Proton-to-Proton encryption by default.
  • You are content to use the Bridge application for desktop IMAP clients.
  • You want a service that has run at scale for over a decade.

Choose VF Mail if

  • You want the whole stack (server, shield and address) on metal one company holds inside the EU, under Maltese and EU law.
  • You want standard IMAP and SMTP for any client without a bridge.
  • You want keys that never leave your hardware and a provider with no free tier, whose only business model is the service fee.
  • You want a custom domain included from the first tier and a founder's licence for Early Access feedback.
  • You want the Permanence Guarantee, two-of-two signed releases and a Terms § 16 warranty enforceable in Malta.
  • You are willing to run a beta and help VF finish it. The paid launch is November 1.

QUESTIONS

Asked.
Answered.

Is VF Mail available today?

Yes, in Early Access. The Windows and Linux builds are downloadable now from the VF Mail page, free while VF polishes the release for the paid launch on November 1. macOS arrives with that launch. The first @vfempire.com inboxes already run on VF's own node, so the system carries real correspondence before it carries yours. Feedback during Early Access earns a founder's licence.

Does VF Mail work with Apple Mail, Thunderbird or Outlook?

Yes. VF Mail exposes full IMAP and SMTP, so any standard client connects directly without a bridge application. VF also ships its own web and mobile clients in the house style: quiet, fast and structurally incapable of advertising to you. Native apps sit on the roadmap; the protocol access is there from the first release.

Where is my VF Mail data stored, and under which law?

On EU metal in Malta that VF Empire holds, encrypted at rest. VF Empire Corp Ltd is a Maltese company, so the service sits under Maltese and EU law, and the Terms § 16 warranty is enforceable in Malta courts. Proton, by its own account, stores data in Switzerland under Swiss law; the two jurisdictions are different choices, and the table above states each without ranking them.

Can I move from Proton Mail to VF Mail?

Proton documents an export tool for downloading your messages, and VF Mail's requirements list one-click import from Gmail, Fastmail and Proton. That importer is planned rather than shipped, so during Early Access the reliable path is a standard IMAP transfer. When you leave VF Mail the door opens the same way: full export over standard protocols, with no hostage data.

How much does VF Mail cost compared with Proton Mail?

VF publishes planned launch pricing on the product page: €3.99 a month for one mailbox with a custom domain, €9.99 for a five-mailbox family plan, €4.99 a seat for teams, with a thirty-day trial instead of a free tier and no renewal games. VF confirms final figures at each release. Proton publishes its own plans and prices on its own site; this page does not restate them.

Is VF Mail open source?

The licence is proprietary and the source is public, the same posture VF applies across its products. The vfmail-desktop repository is scanned for secrets on every push and listed on the security page. If VF ever discontinues VF Mail, the Permanence Guarantee releases the source under a permissive open-source licence within 90 days, so your inbox stays readable long after VF stops building.
