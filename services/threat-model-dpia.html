WHY IT EXISTS

Two documents,
both under-produced across the industry.

Threat modelling and Data Protection Impact Assessment are both legally significant, both routinely produced badly, and both expected to be regulator-ready when needed. Most product teams either skip them, produce them at a token level, or produce them once at launch and never update — and then scramble when a supervisory authority asks.

F·01

Threat models nobody reads

Most threat-model documents are one-off compliance artefacts written to check a box, unread by anyone after week two. The threats aren't ranked. The mitigations aren't cited. The residual risk is not stated. A regulator asking "what did you do about supply-chain compromise?" gets a blank answer.

F·02

DPIAs that pretend the answer is always "low"

The temptation to rate every risk "low likelihood, low severity" so no Art. 36 consultation is triggered. Regulators can tell. IDPC, BfDI, CNIL, DPC, Garante have all rejected DPIAs that fail to name real threats honestly.

F·03

Special-category data quietly ignored

Camera footage may capture health information (visible medical device). Payments may reveal religious affiliation (halal food purchases). Location data may reveal political attendance (protest movements). Art. 9 special-category rules apply — and most DPIAs skip the analysis entirely.

F·04

No named adversary tiers

Threat models that talk about "attackers" abstractly are useless. Real threat models name the adversary — nation-state (Tier 1), hostile insider (Tier 2), sub-processor compromise (Tier 5) — with capability + motivation + what you protect against + what you don't. Regulators + auditors want that specificity.

GDPR Art. 35 DPIA — full structure

10-15 identified risks with severity (L/M/H) + likelihood (L/M/H) + mitigations + residual-risk assessment. No pretending everything is "low." Regulators trust honest DPIAs; they reject the ones that flatten everything to "low likelihood."

COMMON QUESTIONS

What most first calls ask.

Isn't this what our lawyer / GDPR consultant should do?

Legal counsel + GDPR consultants are typically strong on the legal-analysis dimension and weaker on the technical-threat-modelling dimension — most cannot enumerate adversary tiers with technical accuracy or defend the "no third-party AI on user-data paths" claim as an actual product boundary. Our engagement fills exactly that gap: we produce documents that are technically defensible + legally reviewed. Your counsel should still review the final deliverable against your specific circumstances.

What if we already have a threat model / DPIA?

We'll gap-analyse it against what a regulator would expect + tell you honestly whether it needs a full rewrite or a targeted update. Some existing documents need 20% work; some need 100% rewrite. We quote transparently before starting.

Do you produce these in languages other than English?

Primary drafting in English. German + French + Italian + Maltese available as separate translation engagement (~€2-4k per document per language). Malta IDPC accepts English DPIAs; other supervisory authorities may prefer their national language for formal filings.

Will you publish the deliverable / can it be commercially confidential?

The deliverable is yours. Confidentiality mode default — nothing published unless you ask. Some clients ask us to help them publish the threat model as a trust signal (as VF Empire does for its own products); that's a follow-on advisory conversation, not part of the default engagement.

Can this deliverable be filed with a supervisory authority?

Yes. The DPIA is drafted in Art. 35 structure and can be filed directly. If formal Art. 36 consultation is warranted (Extended tier includes preparation packet), we can help you manage the engagement — quoted separately.

What's the difference between this and Service 01 (CRA Readiness Audit)?

Service 01 audits your product against CRA obligations and produces a gap analysis + remediation roadmap. This service (Service 02) produces the actual threat model + DPIA documents you'll need for CRA + GDPR compliance. Many clients start with Service 01 to understand what they need, then engage Service 02 to produce it.

NDA + payment terms?

Standard mutual NDA within 24 hours of request. Invoice in EUR by VF Empire Corp Ltd (Malta) or Noirda GmbH (Germany), 50% at kick-off, 50% at delivery, SEPA transfer.
