WHY IT EXISTS

Two documents,
both under-produced across the industry.

Threat modelling and Data Protection Impact Assessment are both legally significant, both routinely produced badly, and both expected to be regulator-ready when needed. Most product teams either skip them, produce them at a token level, or produce them once at launch and never update, then scramble when a supervisory authority asks.

F·01

Threat models nobody reads

Most threat-model documents are one-off compliance artefacts written to check a box, unread by anyone after week two. They leave the threats unranked, the mitigations uncited, and the residual risk unstated. A regulator asking "what did you do about supply-chain compromise?" gets a blank answer.

F·02

DPIAs that pretend the answer is always "low"

Teams are tempted to rate every risk "low likelihood, low severity" so that no Art. 36 consultation is triggered. Regulators can tell. IDPC, BfDI, CNIL, DPC, Garante have all rejected DPIAs that fail to name real threats.

F·03

Special-category data quietly ignored

Camera footage may capture health information (visible medical device). Payments may reveal religious affiliation (halal food purchases). Location data may reveal political attendance (protest movements). Art. 9 special-category rules apply, and most DPIAs skip the analysis.

F·04

No named adversary tiers

Threat models that talk about "attackers" in the abstract are useless. Real threat models name the adversary (nation-state as Tier 1, hostile insider as Tier 2, sub-processor compromise as Tier 5) with capability + motivation + what you protect against + what you don't. Regulators + auditors want that specificity.

Health, biometric, political-adjacent, religious-affiliation-adjacent, sexuality-adjacent processing. Formal Art. 36 consultation with the supervisory authority is materially more likely, and the DPIA is the primary document the authority reads.

Firms scoping new EU market entry

US or non-EU firms preparing to enter the EU market face GDPR + CRA obligations that they may not have documented in comparable form at home. The threat model + DPIA becomes the foundation of EU-market compliance evidence.

Firms rebuilding after a regulator finding

If you've had a supervisory-authority finding, remediation typically requires an honest DPIA + threat model produced quickly. Standard consulting firms take 3-6 months + charge €80-150k for the same work.

Primary threats ranked by residual risk

8-12 primary threats named + STRIDE-classified + vector-analysed + current-defence-inventoried + residual-risk-rated (VERY LOW / LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH / CATASTROPHIC). Each threat traceable to your specific product architecture.

01 · THREAT MODEL

Threat-driven test scenarios

10-15 concrete test scenarios that map to the primary threats: the QA + audit test cases that verify the defences work. Ready for your CI + third-party-audit plans.

02 · DPIA

GDPR Art. 35 DPIA: full structure

Nature/scope/context/purposes description; legal basis analysis (Art. 6 + Art. 9 where applicable); necessity + proportionality assessment; retention analysis with specific durations + legal basis for each; DPO involvement + supervisory-authority consultation planning.

02 · DPIA

Risks + mitigations rated without softening

10-15 identified risks with severity (L/M/H) + likelihood (L/M/H) + mitigations + residual-risk assessment. We do not pretend everything is "low." Regulators trust honest DPIAs and reject the ones that flatten every risk to "low likelihood."

02 · DPIA

Art. 36 consultation determination

Explicit reasoning on whether formal Art. 36 supervisory-authority consultation is warranted, including our recommendation on which supervisory authority to engage (Malta IDPC as home authority, or the primary authority in your one-stop-shop jurisdiction).

COMMON QUESTIONS

What most first calls ask.

Isn't this what our lawyer / GDPR consultant should do?

Legal counsel + GDPR consultants are typically strong on legal analysis and weaker on technical threat modelling: most cannot enumerate adversary tiers with technical accuracy or defend the "no third-party AI on user-data paths" claim as a product boundary. Our engagement fills that gap with documents that are technically defensible + legally reviewed. Your counsel should still review the final deliverable against your specific circumstances.

What if we already have a threat model / DPIA?

We'll gap-analyse it against what a regulator would expect + tell you whether it needs a full rewrite or a targeted update. Some existing documents need 20% work; some need a 100% rewrite. We quote before starting.

Do you produce these in languages other than English?

Primary drafting in English. German + French + Italian + Maltese available as separate translation engagement (~€2-4k per document per language). Malta IDPC accepts English DPIAs; other supervisory authorities may prefer their national language for formal filings.

Will you publish the deliverable / can it be commercially confidential?

The deliverable is yours. Confidential by default: we publish nothing unless you ask. Some clients ask us to help them publish the threat model as a trust signal (as VF Empire does for its own products); that is a follow-on advisory conversation outside the default engagement.

Can this deliverable be filed with a supervisory authority?

Yes. We draft the DPIA in Art. 35 structure so you can file it directly. If formal Art. 36 consultation is warranted (Extended tier includes preparation packet), we can help you manage the engagement, quoted separately.

What's the difference between this and Service 01 (CRA Readiness Audit)?

Service 01 audits your product against CRA obligations and produces a gap analysis + remediation roadmap. This service (Service 02) produces the threat model + DPIA documents you'll need for CRA + GDPR compliance. Many clients start with Service 01 to understand what they need, then engage Service 02 to produce it.

NDA + payment terms?

Standard mutual NDA within 24 hours of request. Invoice in EUR by VF Empire Corp Ltd (Malta) or Noirda GmbH (Germany), 50% at kick-off, 50% at delivery, SEPA transfer.
